This report describes the disruption that affected the Brackley distribution centre operated by Halvorsen Logistics on Tuesday 14 October 2025, and was prepared by the site operations manager, Priya Okonkwo, together with the IT service desk. It is a fictional document written to measure the quality of the Silicium search. The incident was tracked under the master ticket INC-20417, with the related tickets INC-20418 (conveyor control) and INC-20422 (customer communication).

At 05:42 the night shift supervisor, Daniel Mercer, noticed that the automated sorting conveyor in hall C had stopped and that the handheld scanners in the same hall displayed the message "WMS unreachable". Inbound trucks continued to arrive at docks 7 to 12, but no parcel could be scanned or assigned to an outbound route. Mercer called the on-call engineer at 05:51 and opened INC-20417 at 05:55 with priority P1.

The on-call network engineer, Tomasz Wilk, connected remotely at 06:08 and found that the core switch serving hall C, a unit installed in 2019 and identified as SW-C-CORE-01, was rebooting in a loop roughly every four minutes. Because the redundant switch SW-C-CORE-02 had been taken out of service on 2 October for a firmware upgrade that was never completed, there was no failover path, and the whole hall lost connectivity to the warehouse management system.

Between 06:15 and 07:40 the site switched to the manual fallback procedure. Staff printed paper pick lists from the backup laptop in the shift office and sorted parcels by hand using the colour-coded route labels. Throughput dropped from the usual 4,800 parcels per hour to about 1,150 parcels per hour. Sixty-two agency workers were called in for the morning shift to absorb the backlog, at an additional cost of 9,920 pounds.

A replacement switch was taken from the spare parts store at 07:20, configured from the last saved backup, and installed at 08:05. Connectivity to the warehouse management system was restored at 08:17 and the conveyor was restarted at 08:31 after a safety check. Ticket INC-20418 was closed at 08:40. Full throughput was reached again at 11:10, once the backlog of approximately 14,300 parcels had been processed.

The root cause was a failed power supply unit in SW-C-CORE-01, which caused brown-out reboots. The impact was made worse by the missing redundancy: the change request CHG-3381 for the firmware upgrade of SW-C-CORE-02 had been approved for a single night but was left open for twelve days without anyone noticing, because the change calendar did not send reminders for expired maintenance windows.

The direct financial impact of the incident is estimated at 41,600 pounds. This includes the 9,920 pounds of agency labour, 18,450 pounds of contractual penalties paid to two retail customers for late deliveries, 7,830 pounds of express courier costs to deliver priority orders the same day, and 5,400 pounds for the replacement switch and urgent support from the hardware vendor. Indirect costs such as overtime of permanent staff were not included.

Customer communication was handled under INC-20422. A first notification was sent to the twelve affected key accounts at 06:45, an update at 09:00, and a closing message at 12:30. In total 1,870 orders missed their planned delivery slot, of which 1,420 were delivered later the same day and 450 on the following morning. No order was lost.

The review meeting held on 17 October agreed on the following corrective actions. First, the redundant switch SW-C-CORE-02 is to be put back into service by 24 October, owner Tomasz Wilk. Second, the change management tool must automatically alert the change owner and the site manager when a maintenance window expires with an open change; this is tracked as PRB-0915 and is due by 30 November. Third, the power supply units of all core switches older than five years will be replaced during the first quarter of 2026, with a budget of 22,000 pounds. Fourth, the manual fallback procedure will be rehearsed twice a year, the next drill being scheduled for 20 January 2026.

The incident was classified as a major incident with a total duration of five hours and twenty-eight minutes, measured from the first detection at 05:42 to the return to normal throughput at 11:10. No injuries or safety events occurred during the disruption. Master ticket INC-20417 was closed on 18 October 2025 after the review report was approved by the regional director, Helen Ashworth.
